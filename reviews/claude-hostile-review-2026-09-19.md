# Adversarial whole-site review: 1812: The Whole Story

Review of repository `k3jph/warof1812` at commit `65a7cec` (branch head, 18 September 2026). Review date: 19 to 20 September 2026. No repository files were edited; nothing was committed or pushed.

## 0. Scope, access, and limits

Read this section before the findings, because it changes how some of them should be weighed.

- **The deployed site could not be reached.** The review environment's egress proxy denied every connection to `war-of-1812-whole-story.k3jph.chatgpt.site` (CONNECT rejected, organization policy). The canonical domain named in the repository, `war1812.jameshoward.us`, does not resolve in DNS at all (the parent `jameshoward.us` does). The review therefore ran against a production build of the repository head (`vinext build`, served by `wrangler dev` on localhost). Because the deployed site is generated from this same repository and uses no server-side content, the local build should be a faithful stand-in for everything except hosting-level behaviour (headers, redirects, the ChatGPT sign-in wrapper in `app/chatgpt-auth.ts`, caching, and the external publisher badge). Any finding that depends on hosting is marked as such.
- **External source links could not be opened.** The proxy also blocked nps.gov, loc.gov, history.navy.mil, archives.gov, canada.ca, avalon.law\.yale.edu, founders.archives.gov, and every other cited host. Liveness of the 78 registry URLs, the GIS source URLs, and the documentary packet URLs was not tested. The review checked that they are well formed and point to the right kind of page; it did not confirm they resolve.
- **Web verification was partial.** The chapter 00 to 09 accuracy audit ran roughly 200 web searches against institutional sources and cites URLs for most verdicts. That exhausted the session's search budget. The chapter 10 to 18 audit, the evidence and quotation audit, and the participation audit were then performed from close reading plus the auditors' knowledge of the documents, without search. Where a verdict below rests on knowledge rather than a checked page, it says so. Several of those verdicts (the Treasury sequence, the eleven articles of Ghent, USS Carolina's destruction, "Alabama Territory" in 1814, the wording of the Fort Jackson, Rush-Bagot, Cochrane, and Jackson texts) were independently confirmed by the lead reviewer from the documents themselves and are stated with confidence.
- **What was tested.** All 800 URLs reachable from the homepage were crawled (768 HTML pages, all returning 200). Every same-page anchor was checked against the ids present on that page. Every data endpoint was downloaded and parsed. Every interactive control was driven in headless Chromium. Desktop (1440), laptop (1280, 1200), tablet (1100, 820), and phone (390) renderings were screenshotted and inspected for the homepage, story index, four chapters, Explore, People, Evidence, Edition, Map, Timeline, Interactives and all four systems, Perspectives, Baltimore, Maryland, Backyard, Sources, About, Data, event and record pages, and the 404 page. The content modules were transpiled and every cross-reference (source ids, chapter slugs, event ids, catalog slugs, perspective evidence refs, packet chapters) was resolved programmatically. All nineteen chapters, the five-minute and twenty-minute versions, the regional pages, the catalog, the human record, the documentary packets, the evidence cases, the perspectives, and the GIS data were read in full.
- **Fonts.** The site uses only system fonts (Georgia and Arial via `--serif` and `--sans` in `app/globals.css`), so the local screenshots are representative of what readers see.

## 1. Overall assessment

**As a public-history website: partly succeeds, with one design failure that undercuts the rest.** The homepage, chapter pages, evidence laboratory, perspectives, documentary reader, and Baltimore exhibition are intentionally designed, typographically confident, consistent, and readable on a phone. A first-time reader understands what the site is within one screen. The failure is that the site's most prominent evidentiary features do not do what they say: section-level evidence links are dead in sixteen chapters, the laboratory has seven broken internal links, the map silently omits the Pacific and Ghent it promises, the Documentary Edition delivers one sentence per document, and the People collection presents eight name lists as a 548-person prosopography. The chrome is finished; the machinery under it is not.

**As a coherent long-form narrative: does not yet pass the site's own test.** The two pilot chapters (Baltimore I, The Easy Conquest That Wasn't) and two of the expanded chapters (Blockade, Plattsburgh) read as one careful author reasoning through a problem. The other fifteen expanded chapters reproduce the pilots' surface (corrective lede, antithesis, quotable close) at a fixed four-paragraph section size and then append eight to eighteen disconnected paragraphs after the chapter's natural ending. The same mechanisms are re-explained in two, three, or four chapters; the results of the climax are announced in three earlier chapters; the Plattsburgh-before-Washington order is never signposted; the designated climax is the thinnest of the four 1814 campaign chapters; the twenty-minute version is an assembly of fragments. The editorial specification says "Excellent AI historical prose is not a passing result." As assembled, the book reads as excellent generated prose around four human chapters. The repair is editorial, not a rewrite, and the standard to edit toward already exists inside the book.

**As a trustworthy historical resource: trustworthy in its narrative scaffold, not yet in its apparatus.** Dates, sequences, votes, offices, treaty terms, and the famous set pieces are handled carefully and, with a short list of exceptions, correctly. The evidence boxes make real distinctions and the prose mostly honours them in place. But the parts of the site that claim documentary precision are the least precise: of nineteen primary-source windows, two are paraphrases in quotation marks, one is not a document, three carry doubtful or absent attributions, three contradict their own fidelity notes, and the single most consequential provenance caveat on the site (the Dolley Madison letter survives only as a later copy) appears nowhere. Two thirds of section-source attachments are institutional gateways or tertiary syntheses; no named scholar's book is in the registry; the catalog's evidence labels are unpopulated; the laboratory's confidence percentages have no method. A general reader will not notice. A specialist will, on the first page they check.

**As a usable digital product: usable, with real defects.** Navigation, search, filters, simulators, the mobile menu, keyboard focus, contrast, and empty states all work. Defects: dead in-page anchors, seven 404 links in the laboratory, an ignored `?event=` parameter on the map, metadata that canonicalizes every page to a domain that does not resolve and gives every page the same social card, packet pages without an h1, an Edition hero that clips on phones, an Explore page 186,000 pixels tall on a phone, an unbranded 404, and an interactive map whose features are invisible to assistive technology.

## 2. BLOCKING

### B1. The documentary apparatus presents reconstructed, truncated, or unidentifiable texts as verbatim documents

- **Routes:** every `/story/[slug]` (the "Primary-source window" at the end of each chapter), every `/edition/[slug]`, and the four citation downloads (Chicago, MLA, BibTeX, RIS) on each packet.
- **Files:** `lib/documentary.ts` (the 19 packets and `documentaryCitation`), `lib/chapter-expansions.ts` and `lib/pilot-chapters.ts` (`voice` fields), `components/DocumentaryReader.tsx`.
- **What is wrong.** Each packet's "Diplomatic reading" is a single sentence of 5 to 23 words, and the site exports scholarly citations for it. Checked one by one:
  - Treaty of Fort Jackson (chapter 10 window; packet `treaty-fort-jackson`): "The Creek nation cede to the United States all the territory belonging to said nation within the territories of the United States." Article 1 of 7 Stat. 120 reads "The United States demand an equivalent for all expenses incurred in prosecuting the war to its termination, by a cession of all the territory belonging to the Creek nation within the territories of the United States, lying west, south, and south-eastwardly, of a line..." The sentence has been recomposed into a Creek declarative. The recomposition erases the feature (a list of United States demands) that the chapter's own argument about coercion rests on. Confirmed from the treaty text.
  - Porter at Nuku Hiva (chapter 07; packet `porter-madisons-island`): "I took possession of this island for the United States, and called it Madison's Island." Porter's printed declaration of 19 November 1813 is a formal instrument ("...have, on the part of the said United States, taken possession of the island called by the natives Nooaheevah... but now called Madison's Island"). The first-person sentence matches no known text. The packet note itself tells readers to "consult a page image before scholarly quotation." Not web-verified.
  - "Vessels building at Sacket's Harbour." (chapter 06; packet `lake-ontario-return`): a five-word column heading with identifier "Lake Ontario vessel returns, 1814" and the NHHC topic page as its URL. No document is identified. A citation is exported for it anyway.
  - McClure on Newark (chapter 08; packet `mclure-newark-report`): "The village was set fire to, and about one hundred and fifty houses were laid in ashes." Not found in McClure's letters to Armstrong of 10 and 13 December 1813; the phrasing and the 150 figure belong to British and Canadian accounts. Identifier "Niagara campaign correspondence"; URL is the Library and Archives Canada research-help page. Not web-verified.
  - British war aims (chapter 11; packet `british-war-aims-1814`): "The military possession of the Lakes is essential to the security of His Majesty's North American dominions," attributed to a "British war aims memorandum, 1814." No such document is identified; the packet's URL is a UK National Archives Discovery keyword search. The real documents are Castlereagh's instructions of 28 July 1814 and the British note of 19 August 1814 (American State Papers: Foreign Relations, vol. 3). Wording unverified.
  - Embargo Act (chapter 00; packet `embargo-act-1807`): the statute reads "laid on all ships and vessels in the ports and places within the limits or jurisdiction of the United States." The site drops "in the ports and places," changes "or" to "and," and its note claims "wording and legal syntax are retained from the enrolled statute." Web-verified against govinfo.
  - Rush-Bagot (chapter 18; packet `rush-bagot-notes`): the notes read "shall henceforth be confined to the following vessels on each side, that is..." The site drops "henceforth," cuts the object, and closes with a full stop. Confirmed.
  - Key (chapter 15; packet `defence-fort-mhenry`): the 1814 broadside reads "O! say can you see by the dawn's early light"; the site prints "O say can you see, by the dawn's early light" while claiming "the broadside's punctuation" is retained.
  - Jackson, 21 September 1814 (chapter 17): genuine words, but cut before "with Great Britain, and no longer, there will be paid the same bounty..." That clause is the limit the packet's own introduction is about. Confirmed.
  - Cochrane, 2 April 1814 (chapter 09) and Madison, 1 June 1812 (chapter 01): genuine, truncated mid-sentence with a full stop and no ellipsis.
  - Baltimore pamphlet (chapter 02): the couplet "The direful Mob was heard to shout, We'll drink their blood! we'll root them out!" is attributed to the pamphlet's title page. The narrative's reported cries ("We'll root out the damn'd tories. We'll drink their blood. We'll eat their hearts.") are confirmed; the title-page couplet could not be checked against a page image.
  - Dolley Madison (chapter 14; packet `dolley-madison-letter`; evidence case 02): the wording is the received text, but the August 1814 original does not survive. The text is known from a copy in Dolley Madison's hand made years later, which is why its immediacy is debated. Neither the chapter, the packet, nor the case file that calls it the "earliest trace" says so.
  - Clean: Macdonough (chapter 13), Ghent Article I (chapter 16), Perry (chapter 05), Hull (chapter 03, minor punctuation).
- **Provenance fields.** Only two packets cite a real statutory identifier (2 Stat. 451; 7 Stat. 120). Seventeen give interpretive web pages, gateways, or invented identifiers ("James Lawrence tradition," "Hull proclamation interpretive record," "Porter journal and official reports," "Ghent negotiating instructions, 1814"). Repositories such as "Canadian War Museum" or "Miller Center" are exhibition or transcription sites, not holdings. Several notes concede this ("consult the archival edition before formal quotation").
- **Why it matters.** The site's identity is that it separates documented fact from later memory and links to the deepest record. A "Documentary Edition" that exports BibTeX for a column heading, and a chapter window that rewrites a treaty into the opposite grammatical voice, will cost the site its credibility with exactly the readers it most wants: teachers, archivists, and the Society's own members. The editorial preflight (item 3) requires every quotation be verified; this shows it was not.
- **Smallest fix.** For each of the 19 texts: identify one witness (edition, page, or archival reference), copy the wording from it, mark every cut with an ellipsis, and replace "Diplomatic reading" with "Excerpt" unless a witness is named. Delete or replace the Sackets Harbor packet. Re-attribute or remove the British war aims and McClure texts. Quote Fort Jackson Article 1 as written. Add one sentence on the Dolley Madison copy to the chapter, the packet, and case 02.

### B2. The People collection is eight name lists rendered as a prosopography, and the generated layer overrides the hand-written one

- **Routes:** `/people`, `/people/[slug]` (548 pages), `/explore` (635-record search), every chapter's "People, places, ships, and objects" grid, `/data/people.json`.
- **Files:** `lib/human-record.ts` (eight `seeds`, each with a pipe-delimited `names` string and one set of group-level `places`, `events`, `unit`, `allegiances`, `identityConfidence`, `confidenceNote`, `archiveReason`, `sourceRefs`, `chapter`), `lib/catalog.ts` (the wrapper that writes the template summary "X is preserved here as an index-level lead within..." and `evidence: "Unresolved"`), `components/HumanRecordIndex.tsx`, `components/HumanDetail.tsx`, `components/RelatedRecords.tsx`.
- **What is wrong.**
  - 524 of 548 people records are generated. Across all 548, there are exactly eight distinct values of places, events, unit, allegiances, confidence note, archive reason, source list, and movements. Zero records carry dates. Identity confidence is assigned per group, not per person: the Political, Army, Naval, and British groups are all "Index-level match"; the Baltimore, Indigenous, Black, and Women groups are all "Strong match." So James Madison, Isaac Brock, William Hull, Perry, Porter, Macdonough, and Jackson are labelled INDEX in the workbench, while every one of 82 Baltimore names, including "Hillabee Chief" in the Indigenous list, is STRONG.
  - The 24 hand-written dossiers are overridden because every core slug also appears in a seed and `HumanDetail.tsx` prefers seed metadata. Rendered results: James Madison, "Index-level match... full identity reconciliation remains a research task"; Andrew Jackson, geographic movement "United States, Great Lakes, Chesapeake"; Isaac Brock, "Connected geography: Chesapeake"; Thomas Macdonough, no Lake Champlain; Laura Secord, "Baltimore, Washington, Chesapeake"; Grace Wisher, "Places attached to this identity: Baltimore, Washington, Chesapeake." The page prints the index-record caveat beside dossiers that are not index records.
  - 26 names occur in more than one seed. `catalog.ts` keeps the first occurrence; `humanRecordBySlug` keeps the last. Results: Peter Parker, the Royal Navy captain killed at Caulk's Field, becomes a "Named Maryland official, defender, rioter, printer, privateer, laborer, or witness"; Pierre Lacoste and Louis Daquin, the white commanders of the free-coloured battalions, become "Named Black participant, freedom seeker, militiaman, mariner, or borderlands actor"; William McIntosh and John Norton show one group in the summary and another in the hero.
  - Duplicates and non-persons: John Norton, Captain Norton, and Teyoninhokarawen are three records; John Brant, Wabasha, Jesse Duncan Elliott, Thomas Jesup, Charles de Salaberry, John Sherbrooke, William Hamilton Merritt, Susan Decatur, Joseph H. Nicholson, Eliza Monroe Hay, Thomas Tenant each have two; "Runchey," "Hillabee Chief," "Caesar," and "The Owl" are not names; Georgiana Armistead (born 1817), Anne Willing Bingham (died 1801), John Deserontyon (died 1811), and Checote (born 1819) are outside the war, all "Strong match." The integrity check reports zero duplicates because it compares exact strings.
  - The relationship graph is curated for 14 of 548 records (35 edges); the other 534 draw a fallback of same-group names labelled "shared record group."
  - The identity-confidence filter offers seven options; five return "No record matches" because only two values exist.
  - The chapter grids dump whole seed groups onto one chapter: 185 records under The Easy Conquest That Wasn't (the entire US Army and British seeds, including Pakenham and Villeré), 77 under Baltimore II (Roger Brooke Taney, Charles Carroll of Carrollton, and sixty identical "Named Maryland official..." cards), 70 under War for the Interior (every Muscogee, Cherokee, and Choctaw name), 0 under Niagara Again, Everything Changes, and What Changed. On a phone, Baltimore II is 33,296 pixels tall mostly because of this grid.
  - Auto-linking (`LinkedText.tsx`) sends readers from chapter text to index records whose `chapter` field is the seed default, so the five Ghent commissioners link to Why War, Pakenham and Prévost link to The Easy Conquest, Paul Jennings links to New Orleans. One outright mislink: "William Thornton's argument" in chapter 14 (Dr Thornton of the Patent Office) links to the British-service record for Colonel Thornton of the 85th Foot.
  - `/data/people.json` publishes all 548 as "Public biographical records with roles, significance, evidence labels, chapters, and sources." The interactives page invites readers to "Search hundreds of named people; inspect shared events, units, places, ties, identity confidence." A search for "Ghent" returns all 62 political names because the group's places are ["Washington", "Ghent", "London"].
- **Why it matters.** This is the site's largest collection and the one whose apparatus (confidence badges, allegiance records, movement timelines, a relationship graph, an "archive visibility" chart) most resembles research. All of it is group constants. The disclaimers on the page are honest, and they sit under chrome that says the opposite. The generated layer also degrades the genuine layer, so the site's own best records display false geography. The open-data export then distributes the problem.
- **Smallest fix.** Exclude `corePersonSlugs` from `humanIndexRecords` lookups (or gate on `detail === "dossier"`); render index records without movement, allegiance, event, and confidence fields; hide the graph when `relationships.length === 0`; give each name one group; merge the duplicate pairs and delete the four impossible names and four non-names; restrict `RelatedRecords` to records the chapter actually mentions; have `LinkedText` skip `evidence === "Unresolved"` records; relabel `/people` as "24 dossiers and a 524-name research index" and say so in `people.json`.

## 3. MAJOR

### M1. Factual errors and overstatements in the chapters and events corpus

All are one-line edits. Verification status is given for each.

- **Chapter 03, The Easy Conquest That Wasn't [6.2]:** "His force included New York militia, federal regulars, and Seneca allies." No Indigenous warriors fought on the American side at Queenston Heights; the Buffalo Creek Seneca held to neutrality until 1813. Web-verified (Canadian Encyclopedia, Brock University exhibit). [8.2] then places divided Haudenosaunee kinship "at Queenston," which belongs to 1813 and 1814. Delete "and Seneca allies"; move the kinship point to chapter 12.
- **Chapter 07, Cape Horn [5.19]:** "The British renamed the captured Essex." The Royal Navy commissioned her as HMS Essex, kept the name, and hulked her at Cork in 1823. Web-verified (DANFS; HMS Essex record).
- **Chapter 11, Everything Changes [3.3]:** "William Jones at the Treasury and later Alexander Dallas." Jones was acting secretary only from May 1813 to February 1814; George W. Campbell held the office from 9 February to 5 October 1814, through the failed loan and the bank suspensions; Dallas took office 6 October 1814. Confirmed.
- **Chapter 12, Niagara Again [3.1]:** "losses at the Thames, Moraviantown, and other actions." One battle, two names. Confirmed.
- **Chapter 16, Peace at Ghent [7.16]:** "ten articles to be parsed." The treaty has eleven. Confirmed.
- **Chapter 17, New Orleans [3.3]:** "British artillery damaged or drove off Carolina." Hot shot set Carolina afire on 27 December 1814 and she exploded; the sloop Louisiana was warped out of range. Confirmed.
- **Events corpus,** **`treaty-fort-jackson`****:** place "Alabama Territory." Created 3 March 1817; in 1814 it was Mississippi Territory, which the corpus uses correctly for Fort Mims. Confirmed.
- **Chapter 03 [4.1]:** "Brock secured the provincial legislature's support for emergency measures." The Assembly passed a militia amendment but refused the suspension of habeas corpus; Brock prorogued it and acted through the Executive Council. Overstated. Knowledge-based (Dictionary of Canadian Biography).
- **Chapter 06, Lakes and Shipyards [2.3]:** "American defenders burned stores and a vessel under construction" at Sackets Harbor. The stores were lost; the fire aboard General Pike was put out and she was launched on 12 June 1813. Misleading as written. Web-verified.
- **Chapter 06 [4.1] and [4.2]:** William Bell "oversaw British building" at Kingston in 1814 (the 1814 frigates were built under the contractor John Goudie and Royal Navy shipwrights; Bell was the Provincial Marine's builder); New Orleans and Chippewa "each intended to carry 130 guns" (Chippewa is given as 106 by DANFS; New Orleans 120 to 130). Web-verified.
- **Chapter 01, Why War [2.1]:** "Most Federalists opposed the bill." All thirty-nine Federalists in Congress voted no. Underqualified. Web-verified.
- **Chapter 02, Baltimore I [3.1]:** Lingan "confined on the British prison ship Jersey." Family tradition; Lingan was exchanged in 1780, the year Jersey entered prison service. [3.2] "People outside were killed" is plural for one documented death (Thaddeus Gale). Web-verified (Journal of the American Revolution biography).
- **Chapter 12 [6.13]:** Porter's command at Lundy's Lane "included New York militia and Indigenous allies." Most Haudenosaunee left after Chippawa, as the chapter's own [3.3] says. [5.1] "night of August 15" for an assault that went in before dawn on 15 August. Knowledge-based.
- **Chapter 09, Blockade [7.4], [8.1], [4.2]:** "About two hundred" Colonial Marines is a mid-1814 snapshot (the corps grew to several hundred and roughly 574 settled in Trinidad); "about 1,100 commissions" counts commissions, not the roughly 515 to 526 vessels the cited NPS page describes as "more than 500 privateers"; "July 1813" taxes were enacted in July and August; "$32.5 million" is the WHHA essay's aggregation of a $25 million loan plus Treasury notes. Web-verified.
- **Chapter 05, War for the Interior [6.14]:** Perry "died in 1819 while serving in the Caribbean," which hides yellow fever and the Venezuela mission. Web-verified.
- **Chapter 07 [4.1]:** Phoebe and Cherub "encountered" Essex at Valparaíso in March; they arrived on 8 February and blockaded her for seven weeks. Web-verified.
- **Events corpus:** `cochrane-proclamation` place "British Atlantic command" (dateline is Bermuda); `fort-erie` participants "Haudenosaunee communities" (marginal by August 1814); `north-point` coordinates labelled "site" sit at the landing, about 9 km from the battlefield near Bear Creek; `pensacola` participants "Creek and Black allies" without saying they were on the British side; `st-michaels` omits the second attack of 26 August 1813. `pensacola` also has `chapter: new-orleans` while being listed under Southern Borderlands' related events.
- **Baltimore exhibition (`lib/baltimore.ts`):** "14 September · night" for the Ferry Branch attempt that occurred around 1 a.m. on the 14th (night of 13 to 14); "27 months" between the two summers is 26.8 or 25.5 depending on the start date.
- **Five-minute page, section 06:** "Macdonough arranged his squadron so damaged ships could rotate." The springs and kedges were laid to wind the flagship Saratoga; Eagle shifted by cutting her cable. Not a squadron-wide system.

### M2. Section-level sourcing is dominated by gateways and tertiary pages, and the registry contains no scholarship

- **Routes:** every chapter's "Evidence for this section" and "Sources for this chapter"; `/sources`.
- **Files:** `lib/content.ts` (registry), section `sourceRefs` in `lib/chapter-expansions.ts` and `lib/pilot-chapters.ts`, `lib/source-guide.ts`.
- **Evidence.** Of 436 section-source attachments, 148 (33 percent) are research-guide landing pages, archive gateways, catalogue home pages, or search URLs (LAC research help, NARA gateway, LOC Manuscript Reading Room, Chronicling America "about," LOC maps and pictures home pages, a WorldCat search string attached eight times, a UK Discovery search attached three times); 242 (55 percent) are NPS, NHHC, Parks Canada, Ontario Heritage Trust, WHHA, CWM, or State Department interpretive pages; 33 (7 percent) reach a document, record set, or object; 13 (2 percent) are studies. Four sections are sourced only to gateways: 00 §4 (embargo enforcement, with specific acts named), 06 §5 (dockyard labour), 11 §3 (the Treasury), 16 §1 (the commissioners). Chapter 07 builds its argument on Porter's published journal and never cites it. Chapter 04 §6 discusses Dartmoor with `nhhc`, `loc-pictures`, and WorldCat while `nps-dartmoor` and `loc-dartmoor` sit unused in the registry. Chapter 17 §8 sources Louaillier, Judge Hall, and the contempt fine to three pages about Black service, Jordan Noble, and legacies. No monograph by Hickey, Stagg, Taylor, Latimer, Benn, Malcomson, Skaggs, Sugden, Waselkov, Gilje, or Sheads appears anywhere.
- **Also:** the research guide's groups omit 11 of the 78 registry sources (the Jefferson letter, both WHHA portrait pages, the Army campaign study, Ontario Heritage, Parks Canada Queenston, the Senate Ghent page, NPS Trade and Commerce, WHHA war finance, and the two rights pages), so `/sources` reports "67 distinct resources." Four sources (`nps-charles-ball`, `nps-elizabeth-sands`, `nps-dartmoor`, `loc-dartmoor`) are cited nowhere, and they are exactly the named-person evidence the participation gaps below need.
- **Why it matters.** The About page promises "Every major claim should lead somewhere." A gateway is not somewhere. The pattern is citation laundering by volume: three chips per section look like support and mostly are not.
- **Smallest fix.** Attach at least one document-level or scholarly source to every section that states a number, a quotation, or a named individual's act; add the standard monographs and the documentary editions (Dudley and Crawford; Cruikshank; American State Papers; Kappler) to the registry; put the eleven missing sources into guide groups; cite the four orphaned sources where their subjects appear.

### M3. The evidence laboratory renders unexplained percentages and a misapplied state

- **Routes:** `/evidence`, `/evidence/[slug]`, and the case callouts on chapter pages.
- **Files:** `lib/evidence-lab.ts`, `app/evidence/page.tsx`, `app/evidence/[slug]/page.tsx`, `components/EvidenceCases.tsx`.
- **What is wrong.** Confidence is shown as 96, 94, 99, 91, and 89 percent with progress bars, with no method and a different referent in each case: case 03 "New Orleans won the war" is "True, with limits, 99%" although its own verdict is a documented negative ("It did not force the Treaty of Ghent"); case 05 is "Unsupported as stated, 89%," where the number measures confidence in the negative verdict. Four of five ledgers are entirely tertiary (NPS and WHHA pages); case 04 cites no Armistead report, Key manuscript, or British log; case 03 does not attach the treaty text or the Senate page that are in the registry; case 05 refers to "unit records and contemporary descriptions reviewed by historians" without naming one. Case 01's evidence note misparaphrases Jefferson's letter (it predicts Canada "as far as the neighborhood of Quebec" this year and Halifax next, not "Quebec could be attacked in the next campaign"); chapter 03 [1.4] adds a gloss ("once the British attack on the United States united American opinion") that is not in the letter.
- **Why it matters.** The laboratory is the site's showpiece for evidence discipline. False precision on its verdict stamps undermines the careful prose beneath them.
- **Smallest fix.** Replace the percentages and bars with the site's own categories (documented, strong inference, plausible, later recollection, tradition, disputed, unknown) applied to the verdict; attach at least one primary trace to each ledger; correct the Jefferson paraphrase.

### M4. Narrative architecture: addendum tails, spoilers, and an unsignposted chronology

- **Routes:** `/story/[slug]` for all seventeen expanded chapters; `/twenty-minutes`.
- **Files:** `lib/chapter-expansions.ts`; `app/twenty-minutes/page.tsx`.
